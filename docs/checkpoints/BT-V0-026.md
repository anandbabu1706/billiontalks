# BT-V0-026 — Guest Publisher Recovery After Background Resume

Follows BT-V0-025. Test 51 production diagnostics showed that the guest remained `JOINED` after returning from the mobile browser background, while `publisherSessionPresent` was `false`, `publishedTracks` was empty, and the host excluded the guest with `publisher-session-missing`. The host publisher remained healthy.

## Findings

- Publisher health was not checked when the page returned from the background. A browser PeerConnection could still appear connected even though its Cloudflare publisher session was gone.
- The browser's local published-track map could remain stale while the server no longer had the publisher session.
- The prior recovery path scheduled capture reconciliation and republishing asynchronously. Recovery could be reported complete while capture or publish-ready was still pending.
- Test 51's server-side evidence establishes the missing server publisher session, but cannot by itself establish whether the guest issued `/media/publish` or `/media/publish/ready` during that production interval.

## Recovery Changes

- Coalesced `visibilitychange`, `pageshow`, `focus`, and `online` resume signals after a recorded meeting background/offline transition, avoiding duplicate recovery requests.
- Resume performs a bounded publisher rebuild and waits for local media validation, `/media/publish`, the Cloudflare answer, and `/media/publish/ready` before reporting success.
- Live, unmuted microphone and camera tracks are reused. Ended or muted tracks are reacquired for devices that remain enabled.
- A stale 410 during resumed publication triggers one additional publisher-session reset and republish attempt; further retries are stopped.
- Recovery verifies that the publisher session is present and all expected active local track names are in the ready publication set.
- The same admitted participant identity is retained; recovery does not create or rejoin a participant row.

## Diagnostics and Regression Coverage

Diagnostics include background/resume timestamps and trigger, local track ready state and mute state, capture reuse/reacquisition outcome, publisher recovery start/end and result, publish and publish-ready status, resulting publisher-session presence, and ready track names. They do not expose credentials or bearer tokens.

Regressions cover live-track reuse, ended-track reacquisition, stale-410 bounded retry, publish-ready-before-success, coalesced resume events, admitted guest identity continuity, server recovery under the same participant, host rediscovery of guest microphone and camera, and avoiding duplicate participant rows.
